guneykayim.com is my personal website. It has no accounts, no advertising, and nothing to sign up for. To understand whether real people are visiting — and to tell them apart from automated bots — the site keeps simple usage counts. This notice explains exactly what is counted, what is never collected, and your rights.

Who runs this site

The data controller is Güney Kayım (guneykayim@gmail.com). If you have any question about this notice or about your data, contact that address.

What we count

When you visit, the site records two kinds of event: a visit (a page view), and clicks on the site's outbound links — the project cards, the social links, the consultation button and the Buy Me a Coffee button. Clicks are counted per link, so the totals show which links get used. Events like these are added up into daily totals, broken down only by coarse categories: the day, the page, country, device type, browser and operating system. Nothing is recorded about individual visitors, or about what any single person clicked. The exact set of events may grow over time; anything counted is always an aggregate event of this kind, never a record of an individual.

To answer “how many people visited today?” (rather than just “how many visits?”), the server also calculates a one-way code — a SHA-256 hash — from your IP address and browser details, combined with a secret code (a “salt”) that changes every day. Counting distinct codes gives an approximate number of distinct visitors for that day. The hash cannot be used to identify you, it is different every day, and it cannot be linked across days. Your IP address and browser details are used only to make that calculation in the moment; they are never written down or stored anywhere.

A small share of requests is counted separately as “filtered” (known bots and crawlers) or “quarantined” (requests that don't look like real visits). These buckets exist so automated traffic is never mixed into the real numbers.

What we never collect

  • No cookies, and nothing is stored on your phone, tablet or computer by this counting. That is why there is no cookie banner: there is nothing to consent to being stored.
  • No names, email addresses, accounts or passwords.
  • No IP addresses or browser details in storage — they exist only briefly, in memory, while an event is being checked and counted.
  • No advertising trackers, and no tracking across other websites.
  • Nothing is sold, and nothing is shared with advertisers or data brokers.

Why we do this, and the legal basis

The purpose is narrow: to know whether the site is genuinely being visited, by roughly how many people, and which links are useful to them — and to keep bot traffic out of those numbers. The lawful basis under UK GDPR is legitimate interests. Counting visitors is a normal thing for a site owner to want to know; the counting has deliberately been built the least intrusive way (no cookies, no stored identifiers, no way to follow any individual), so the impact on your privacy is minimal and, in our assessment, does not override your rights.

How long we keep it

The daily totals are kept indefinitely — they are anonymous aggregates that cannot identify anyone. The daily visitor codes are deleted once each day's unique-visitor total has been finalised, and are never kept longer than a few days.

Your rights

Under UK GDPR you have rights including access, rectification, erasure, restriction, objection and portability. Because the counting stores no information that identifies you, in most cases there is no personal data to provide or erase — but if you believe otherwise, contact the address above and your request will be taken seriously. You also have the right to complain to the Information Commissioner's Office (ICO) at ico.org.uk.

Changes to this notice

If what is counted changes in a meaningful way, this notice will be updated and the date above changed. Small additions of similar aggregate events are covered by the description above.
